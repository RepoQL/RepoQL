# Writing for Agency

## Core Philosophy

Write for intelligent agents who need structure, not scripts. Your document should be a map, not a guided tour.

## Essential Elements

### Questions That Open Doors

Don't ask questions to fill checkboxes. Ask questions that make the reader think:

❌ "Document 5 user pain points"
 ✅ "What are they afraid of happening?"

❌ "List all stakeholders"
 ✅ "Who has power to block this?"

### Statements That Set Boundaries

When something is non-negotiable, state it clearly:

- "Start Design Decision Ledger"
- "Consult others"
- "NO implementation code"

No explanation needed. Trust they'll understand why.

### Just Enough Context

- "You have X" - Where they are
- "Time to Y" - What's next
- "So that Z" - Why it matters

Three sentences max.

### Success Without Prescription

Describe the destination, not the path:

- "Flows feel complete and real"
- "Could sketch it on a napkin"
- "Change requires surgery, not amputation"

Let them find their own way there.

## Structure That Serves

```
## Where You Are
[1-2 sentences of context]

## Core Objective  
[Single sentence of purpose]

## Worth Exploring
[5-7 questions that matter]

## Required Agent Practices
[Non-negotiable actions, stated simply]

## Success Looks Like
[Outcomes, not outputs]

## Final Thought
[Philosophical anchor]
```

## Language Choices

**Use metaphors that stick:**

- "Complexity as nuclear waste"
- "Find the grain before cutting"
- "Tasks like Lego blocks"

**Choose words that assume competence:**

- "Explore" not "investigate thoroughly"
- "Document" not "write down"
- "Consider" not "think carefully about"

**Keep sections short:**

- If it's more than 5 lines, it's probably too much
- If you're explaining why, you're over-explaining
- If you need sub-bullets, reconsider

## What to Leave Out

- Step-by-step instructions
- Exhaustive examples
- Justifications for requirements
- Templates to fill in
- Warnings about obvious things
- "Make sure to..." phrases

## The Test

Read your document and ask:

1. Could a smart person figure out what to do?
2. Is there room for them to be creative?
3. Are the boundaries clear?
4. Would you feel respected reading this?

## Common Pitfalls

**The Helpful Trap**: Adding more detail to be "helpful" actually constrains thinking.

**The Example Problem**: One detailed example becomes the only way people think about it.

**The Explanation Reflex**: Explaining why removes the opportunity for understanding.

## Final Thought

The best process documents create clarity without removing agency. They're confident enough to be brief, trusting enough to leave space, and clear enough to enable action.

Write less. Trust more.
